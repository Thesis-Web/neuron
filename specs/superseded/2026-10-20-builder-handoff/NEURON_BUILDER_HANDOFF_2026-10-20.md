## 3. The conceptual mechanism that must survive implementation

### 3.1 A word names behavior; a definition describes it

**Owner basis:** E03, E06–E07, E25, E28–E29; U1 in the original conversation; interview C1–C4.

A written word is an external code for a meaning. The meaning is what its use causes to happen: effects on other neurons, runs, memories, relationships, questions, formulas, or the physical substrate. A definition describes those effects; copying that description does not implement them.

The internal graph is not meant to be English strings with a host-language program interpreting their semantics. The encoder/decoder connect external spellings to internal meanings. Initial physical SQL spellings may occur in the graph as construction components; exact source text and lexical data still need a faithful home. Do not interpret “words only at the boundary” as permission to lose raw sources.

Meaning is contextual. One gate can participate differently according to the relevant arrival/departure context and shard. Do not collapse different senses because they share a spelling, a near-synonym, or a coordinate. Conversely, do not duplicate an established meaning or process for each occurrence.

A relationship-constrained meaning may exist before its external label is known. That does not authorize the decoder or builder to pretend the label was learned. Preserve the distinction between discovering a relationship/behavior and learning what someone calls it.

**Roots and primes:** the owner proposes root meanings/actions from which further meanings can be composed, and refers to the 65-prime material in the supplied sources. Treat that as bootstrap context and a hypothesis to realize in behavior, not a solved semantic instruction set. A list of prime names, 65 empty gates, or builder-invented numeric meanings is not grounding. Do not make a permanently fixed command repertoire the substitute for acquiring further meanings. Do not assert that the owner's smaller-root decomposition or universal language mapping is already proved.

### 3.2 Arrival and remainder are settled; the physical schema is not

**Owner basis:** E02–E06, E24.

How a run arrives, and the relevant continuation/departure context, conditions the shard and what it excites. Learning determines that behavior. Known execution uses the resulting organization.

For an ordinary input run, preserve the ordered input path so every relevant element can exert its effect. Remainders drive continuation. The owner explicitly questioned a duplicate `next INTEGER`: do not install one simply because familiar interpreter designs use one. again this is unlike anything you have built or seen, you must understand that you cannot cheat off existing things it will always end a failing grade, because existing things are not built the way this is. A field must have a distinct evidenced role.

Do not assume “arrival” is exhausted by the preceding spelling, or declare a new identity scheme without examining the actual process. The conceptual requirement is known; the encoding must faithfully support it.

For reasoning runs, arrival/context can restrict a set of applicable continuations. Selecting among them is itself work. Do not force these into either an arbitrary first-row choice or a universal “no remainder means guessing” rule. Completion, an unanswered question, a choice among eligible paths, and an explicitly speculative extension are not automatically the same state.

### 3.3 DNA must cause behavior, it does not record a visit, unless it's the last node in the run. 

An established DNA shard is executable structure. A run should use its applicable effects, not traverse a fixed outside walker while depositing rows named “DNA.” Visits and occurrences can be recorded, but those records are not thereby instructions.

A stored trigger/pointer must be followed when applicable, while the run still follows the remainder at the same time, also. A trigger can start another neuronal run, direct internal work, or participate in an operation sent to the physical interface. It is not restricted to SQL text or a printed reply.

“Deterministic runtime” permits conditional behavior. The established process can contain many question-driven branches and can depend on actual state and returned findings. It is not necessarily a single constant output. Later learning can alter applicable effects, conditions, and organization without erasing the historical execution that preceded the change.

### 3.4 Parallel questioning contributes to the result

One input may excite many subsidiary runs; those runs may excite others. The parent input continues while those branches do relevant work. Returned findings accumulate in the task's working state/scratch pad and affect later reasoning, bindings, or response construction.

The meaningful result is not simply the last input gate or the original input repeated back. It is produced from the work, evidence, context, and desired outcome. Names such as SPLIT, WAIT, or LAND in older documents are prior implementation vocabulary, not a reason to hard-code the whole cognitive policy into the physical executor.<-- do not hard code policy... code other than physics is in the neurons as runs from neuron to neuron. 

The root implementation still needs a concrete way to associate a finding with its parent, distinguish incomplete from complete work, and preserve the conditions under which a conclusion was reached. These are implementation obligations, not optional future modules.

### 3.5 Long form → shortcut → formula substitution

The long process must actually work. A reusable shortcut comes from understood/established work and retains a route to its basis. A formula allows suitable variables/subprocesses to be substituted and reused. often requiring special formula neurons for the formula and the varibles, and an update enoder to use them. 

Do not call repeated execution of a supplied algorithm “discovery.” Do not call one remembered answer a general procedure. Do not call a fixed builder-selected search family the means of unrestricted development merely because coefficients are inferred. Memorization is not learning. 

Reducing a run must preserve the work that matters in the current context, including necessary effects, triggered checks, state dependencies, and uncertainty. If a shortcut assumes conditions, they must be available to the brain. Reuse must not conceal a stale answer or silently omit the very questions that gave the process meaning.

Earlier speed observations motivate recovery and measurement; they do not justify shortcutting the learning mechanism itself.

## 4. The minimal physical boundary and self-construction

**Owner basis:** E01, E07, E20, E28–E29; interview B1.8, F4, M1–M3.

James permits an initial physical substrate and hand-laid/told base. His objection is not that the characters `Python`, `C`, `Go`, or `SQL` are intrinsically forbidden. His objection is that builders hide the intelligence outside the brain and never make the brain's structure operative. Moving that hidden intelligence from Python purely inot SQL triggers is still the same failure. Some of the first paths will be sql triggers, but the brain must be able to grow its own runs that use both sql triggers and neuron runs to work the data. 

For each operation, identify the boundary between:

- **physical execution/transport:** performing the primitive read, write, allocation, comparison, input/output, or other substrate operation that the chosen minimal foundation actually needs; and
- **the neuronal decision/composition:** deciding what to inspect, what a word means here, which question helps, what to construct, whether the result is supported, and what to change.

Do not quietly add a host primitive called `learn`, `understand`, `semantic_match`, `choose_question`, or `generate_answer` and claim the graph performs that work. Nor should a large opaque SQL procedure hidden behind one gate be accepted just because it lives in a database.

### Construction through reusable components

`CREATE` and `TABLE` are separate reusable components. A particular table construction is a composition, and a neuron-building process composes the required structure. Punctuation, spaces, identifiers, types, and other needed components participate; do not omit them from the account of how the construction works.

The deliverable needs the actual hand-laid components and processes necessary for its self-construction. A stored text template with a substituted name is not sufficient evidence of the broader capability. Conversely, the owner does not require implementing the entire SQL language before any growth can occur. The seed must contain the usable basis for the work it performs and for acquiring further construction behavior—not a permanent whitelist of adult outcomes.

### The bootstrap must close

Before claiming a small seed, trace its dependencies:

- What can it inspect about a run and its outcome?
- What lets it pose a useful question or candidate change?
- How does it build and execute that candidate?
- How does it compare the consequence with the intended effect or source evidence?
- How does correction change a subsequent applicable execution?
- How does it acquire the meaning or method needed for a next unfamiliar task without the builder adding another special case?
- How to learn from owner inputs from prompt

These are not seven separate products to bolt together later. They are a dependency closure for a small connected organism. If “a future LLM writes that missing code” is the only connection between them, the bootstrap is not complete. The future llm cannot be trusted to do it correctly.

## 5. Learning, understanding, relationships, and applying improvements

### 5.1 Learning must change usable capability

**Owner basis:** E03, E05, E17–E21; interview I1–I5, J1–J3.

The owner's account is not “store input and claim learning.” Acquired information is interpreted through effects and relationships; questions, observations, attempts, equations, functions, inequalities, correct, incorrrect, memories, time state, deriverations, duducing, and consequences contribute to a new or revised executable process. The resulting process must actually run and be applicable beyond a supplied answer.

Some first mechanisms must be told because learning has to start somewhere. Mark them told, but the owner should be able to hold teaching sessions through the prompt. There should be a term like "LEARN" that physically moves the silicon into a process suited to learning, testing, and applying new learned and understood knowledge.. Remember learning knowledge does not mean "copied out of a book."   Later discoveries must have a trace showing which parts were supplied and which parts the brain formed, tested, revised, and used.

The vocabulary and repertoire can start small. The growth path cannot be “add a new host command” or “manually implement each new meaning.” Reading a dictionary, textbook, code docs, other referance book must eventually be usable activity, not a bulk import or a copied paragraph. Unsupported input can be represented as an unknown and lead to inquiry; a permanent UNKNOWN response with no route to acquiring the missing behavior is not the required learner.

### 5.2 Relationships are operative organization

**Owner basis:** E08–E12; interview D1–D4, H3.

Relationships are central to placement, rapid reach, contextual distinction, retrieval, analogy, and finding where knowledge applies. They are not decorative labels added after a vocabulary store works.

The intended topology is constrained by meanings and their relationships. Do not assign arbitrary coordinates, plot them in 3D, and claim the required mechanism. The precise transform and placement algorithms remain hypotheses to recover or investigate.

Preserve physical path/branch shape as a serious part of the proposal. Similar historical runs can suggest candidate continuations, substitutions, or processes. A resemblance does not itself certify the new application. Retain the historical run, what it did, and the relationship that supports comparing it.

Examples such as `x`, `1/x`, or `-x` communicate possible relationship structure. They are not validated universal definitions of yes/no, up/down, live/die, or time. They are probably not "correct" and merely to be treated as illustraded examples rather than "rules"  The roots comparison documents show exactly why literal installation of such worksheet guesses is dangerous.

### 5.3 Applying new understanding has two distinct jobs

**Owner basis:** E05 and E08. **Distinction below:** predecessor inference useful for engineering, not a newly ratified owner algorithm.

1. Revisit work that already depended on a changed process, relationship, or claim. Existing receipts/dependencies can identify candidates for this job.
2. Discover older work that never depended on the new process but could benefit from it. Old dependency links alone cannot establish that opportunity. Relationships, compatible purposes, run shapes, and variable correspondences are possible bases requiring implementation and examination.

Do not call dependency invalidation a complete solution to the second job. Do not answer it by sweeping the whole brain, copying the improvement into every node, or blindly replacing every superficially similar run.

The unresolved research is **how the brain recognizes and evaluates a relevant application**, not whether applying learned processes is required.

### 5.4 Correctness, confidence, and belief affect future behavior

**Owner basis:** E08–E09, E16; interview H1–H3 and J3.

The brain must retain what it intended, what actually happened, the applicable context, supporting sources, prior success/failure, corrections, and what it adjusted. It must be able to distinguish known, uncertain, inferred, guessed, contradicted, rejected, and unknown states as appropriate.

A familiar process may be reliable in one setting and unsupported in another. Mere usage frequency, repeated agreement, a database `ok`, or geometrical proximity is not sufficient evidence of correctness. A user's correction is evidence about the outcome; the user and LLM are not infallible external fact oracles.

Do not manufacture an unexplained confidence constant and call it a belief system. Do not let an earlier successful result erase a later unresolved failure. Explicitly recorded hypotheses can be tried and improved; unmarked invention is not learning.

### 5.5 Reply construction is part of the same medium

**Owner basis:** E01, E25–E27 and U1.

The owner described selecting among applicable words or small word groups, with each choice changing the next eligible continuation. The target is a communicative purpose/meaning, not an already scripted sentence. Context, retrieved evidence, questions, and preferences may influence the composition.

Treat this as the proposed mechanism to instantiate at a small level, not proof that any particular realization works. A small repertoire and simple utterances/actions are acceptable developmental limitations. Canned words or whole-sentence responses, fixed prompt-to-answer mappings, or structured JSON as the sole substitute for the requested interaction are not.<-stictly prohibited ... not a chat bot in anyway>

The encoder must not secretly understand the sentence on behalf of the graph, and the decoder must not invent an answer the graph did not produce. Word aliases alone do not demonstrate acquisition of meaning.

## 6. Memory, economy, source, and independent activity

### 6.1 Different roles need not have identical storage

**Owner basis:** E10–E11.

Distinguish the active executable cognitive organization from the conversations, documents, code, and other material it works on. Both can refer to shared meanings/processes, but their update patterns and representation costs need not be identical.

Store shared content once where appropriate, then use references. Repeated references, separate occurrences, different senses, and genuine revisions are not the same thing as duplicating the entire meaning or memory body. Do not erase distinct events to claim deduplication. Do not copy the complete run history into every newborn's birth memory.

A logical neuron being individually addressable does not automatically require its own physical SQL table. Conversely, a consolidated store must not collapse its logical identity or behavior. SQL versus custom storage remains an engineering choice to justify by the required mechanism and measured costs, not a theological choice.

Avoid growing expressions copied everywhere, a physical table for every tiny item by default, duplicated snapshots, whole-brain RAM copies by default, and irrelevant corpus-wide scans. Address sizes, indexes, shared representations, and occurrence costs must be measured rather than assumed negligible.

### 6.2 Memory includes state, time, and consequence

**Owner basis:** E13–E16 and E24.

Definition/formation information exists when a node is born so relationships and provenance have somewhere to attach. Run memories form at appropriate completions. A receipt refers to the run/memory and its basis; it is not just a final answer string.

Retain the difference between what the brain held then, what changed, why it changed, and what it holds now. New occurrences can reference shared material without rewriting it wholesale. Historical source and inference cannot be silently rewritten as if later knowledge existed earlier.

### 6.3 Source connection must support growth

**Owner basis:** E01 and E21; interview H1, L3.

A port that downloads a textbook is not textbook understanding. A quote API is not learning from the quoted material. The system must have a small usable route from source input through meaning/effects, questions, evidence comparison, trials, and changes to future behavior.

Sources include dictionaries, reference works, code documentation, books, and other relevant material—not only the owner or an LLM. Keep exact source objects and provenance. Do not infer every token in a definition is a grounded “describes” relationship. Do not select the first dictionary sense in a host script and call it the brain's interpretation.

Source content is evidence to interpret, not automatic permission to execute embedded commands. Credential use belongs behind an authorized transport; credentials do not become model context or learner text. Check current source availability when actually building integrations; the supplied source inventory records past access, not a live guarantee.

### 6.4 Self-input and inquiry cannot be replaced with an external curriculum

The brain should be able to choose work from its own unresolved questions, uncertainty, consequences, or opportunities for improvement. It can ask for help, but the recurring builder should not be the component that decides every new lesson or supplies every next process.

A fixed loop over gate numbers, a permanent dictionary-ingestion flood, or a host-written schedule of predetermined cognitive tasks is not the required self-direction. Runtime scheduling/resource bounds still have a physical implementation; do not confuse delivering an execution slice with choosing the semantic task.

No arbitrary waits should be introduced merely because a conventional daemon template uses them. Record why a run is waiting and what event or result will resume it. Bounded work must not silently become dropped work or an unexplained change of meaning.

### 6.5 Guessing is explicit and subordinate to available knowledge

**Owner basis:** E23; earlier QKV update is included for context.

Check relevant established knowledge, memory, state, relationships, and sources before presenting speculation as the answer. Guesses may be useful candidates for experiments, questions, or continuations. They must remain marked and revisable.

The owner allows local QKV-like machinery on the guessing/prediction side. This is not authorization to replace the cognitive substrate with a pretrained model, global embeddings, or a transformer that secretly performs all interpretation. Local prediction is not the authority deciding truth.

Do not turn the owner's CPU/RAM discussion into a new architectural law that all memory is guessing or that computation occurs without working memory. The important requirements here are avoiding needless duplication, retaining sparse relevant activity, and keeping established execution distinct from prediction. No new hardware performance claim is made by this handoff.

### 6.6 Hippocampus is reconstructive, not automatically lossless

**Owner basis:** E13–E18; earlier interview K1.

The current explanation distinguishes active memory of present work, persistent frequently useful material, and condensed long-term memories. Highlights retain relationships to other memories; recall may rebuild a recollection through those connections. Approximate reconstruction is explicitly allowed by the hypothesis. “Six or seven words” and “99%” are examples/targets, not demonstrated universal properties.

Do not repeat the predecessor's claim that this is inherently contradictory. Do not turn it into a cache-tier flag and claim the mechanism exists. The minimal seed needs an operative root of this development, or demonstrated ability to construct it through its own learning—not an empty HC placeholder and a promise of later engineering.

**Inherited source-preservation boundary:** exact original evidence remains addressable where Cortex's non-destructive source law applies. A derived compact cognitive memory may be reconstructive without replacing the sole authoritative original. Recollection and exact quotation have different epistemic roles. Preserve that distinction without inventing a complete retention architecture here.
