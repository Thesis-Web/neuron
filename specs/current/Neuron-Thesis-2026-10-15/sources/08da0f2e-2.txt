1. What we are building

Neuron’s aim is cognition through persistent, executable, revisable graph structure.

The system should acquire capability from experience, recall relevant experience, compose known processes, investigate missing knowledge, recognize its guesses, revise mistaken relationships, and eventually initiate useful activity itself.

Conversation is an early interface and demonstration. The intended substrate can also support logic, compilers, tools, storage, memory, and other computational processes. Those are intended capabilities whose realization must be demonstrated.

`/neuron2` means Cortex project/book `neuron-v2`, which contains the later V16.2 work. `/neuron` and predecessor experiments provide history. The current implementation location and version must come from a dated current record.

2. How to interpret the owner’s explanations

The owner can describe a process and its intended behavior without knowing its mathematical implementation.

3. Where capability must live

Gate values identify mathematical locations. Surface words and storage identifiers are distinct from those locations.

DNA holds learned local relationships and continuations. Arrival conditions and remaining work determine which continuation applies. Runs compose those operations. The runs themselves are formulas.

The graph must contain the substantive behavior attributed to a capability. A named neuron that calls an external routine for the missing reasoning does not demonstrate that the neuron acquired the capability.

The mother can teach and provide feedback during development. The student must eventually retain and use acquired abilities with the mother disconnected. That includes learning and selection processes when those are the capabilities being claimed.

Every implementation must disclose its supplied primitives and remaining external decision-making. That boundary cannot quietly expand to make a demonstration succeed.

4. Learning, execution, and two kinds of shortcut

Learning does the work of discovering, proposing, testing, placing, connecting, and revising usable structure. Execution uses established structure.

There are two important savings:

- Execution reuse: substitute or execute an applicable established process without rediscovering its internals.
- Learning reuse: reopen a related run, change a useful internal decision, and retain the parts that still apply.

The second is recursive. A shortcut can contain another process whose internal choice becomes relevant to a new problem.

The long route remains recoverable because it contains useful decisions, assumptions, and relationships. It supports correction and invention as well as verification.

A claim must distinguish a supplied procedure, a taught association, a recalled result, a new composition, and a discovered relationship.

5. Receipts, memories, timeline, and local alternatives

A receipt preserves what actually occurred: the route, selected DNA, and sufficient bindings and provenance to recover meaningful decisions. Different derivations can reach the same destination.

Memory preserves experience. Timeline distinguishes when something happened from when it was recalled, reinterpreted, or understood differently.

For adapting a remembered process, the owner’s current procedure is:

> Follow its receipt backward → inspect local DNA with the same completed arrival → compare applicable known continuation outcomes → follow a useful alternative → recursively inspect further where needed.

This investigation should use the local structure already reached. It should not

6. Memory has multiple access routes

Memories can be reached through patterns, relationships, problems, processes, outcomes, definitions, or time.

The owner also describes a composed “feeling” pattern as a rapid entry route: bodily and internal features together identify potentially relevant experiences. The next step checks their composition and applicability.

That is a reported mechanism to investigate. It is not a fixed emotion label or a proven numerical encoding.

Fast retrieval requires learned access structure that reaches relevant candidates. Early rejection helps inspect candidates cheaply, but alone does not explain how the system avoids visiting every memory.

7. Language must lead to usable processes

Words, grammar, punctuation, tense, roles, and context affect how a run is composed and used. We need to establish how, why, where, and when those relationships operate.

The result of a sentence run can be:

- An available answer.
- A question needed to resolve uncertainty.
- A process for obtaining information.
- An action or sequence of actions.
- A combination of these.

An answer location may therefore provide a way to obtain an answer rather than contain a final response.

The shopping example illustrates context-sensitive selection: who is asking, current circumstances, relevant preferences, timing, cost, quality, location, and available evidence can change the appropriate process.

Those details must come from actual state and memories, or remain explicitly unknown. The illustrative dialogue must not become a scripted substitute for that selection.

External maps, calendars, or searches are possible future interfaces. Their presence would not by itself establish cognition; the student’s use and selection of them is part of the capability.

8. Unknown words can have partially known meanings

The scope must distinguish:

- An unknown surface word.
- An unnamed but relationally constrained location.
- A partially known definition.
- A missing relationship.
- A missing route to an existing location.

Relationships can point toward an unnamed location and constrain what belongs there. The system may know substantial properties without knowing the label.

The intended behavior can be:

> “I do not know the word. These relationships suggest this meaning, and these parts remain guesses.”

A printed word already available in the decoder does not demonstrate this capability.

Reasoning uses and composes processes to resolve missing dependencies. Selecting the next question is itself a capability requiring an executable process.

Question words alone do not identify that process. “What caused this?” and “Why did this happen?” may serve similar dependencies.

Guesses enable exploration. Their origin, assumptions, evidence, predictions, and current standing must survive execution and recall. Later evidence may resolve a guess without erasing that it began as one.
