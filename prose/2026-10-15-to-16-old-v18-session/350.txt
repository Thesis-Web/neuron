Why the brain has learned nothing. Every row in brain.db was typed by you, and nothing in it can do anything. Neuron 0 is data. The first neurons should have been the ones that make things. That's the answer to the chicken and the egg: the first egg is the neuron that lays eggs. Compilers start the same way: a tiny piece is written by hand once, and from then on the compiler builds itself.

What only you can lay, once. I tested this on your hub's sqlite:
- A trigger can make, change and delete rows.
- A trigger cannot create a table (syntax error), and it cannot attach another db file.

So the tables and the trigger are the bones. You lay them by hand, once, because the brain can never make them from inside. Neurons, shards, memories and timeline moments are rows, and the brain can make all of those itself.

That changes "create a new dna table for gate xyz". The brain can't ever do that. What it can do is lay gate xyz's first shard and write its first memory, as rows in the one dna table. The "dna inside a neuron" is that neuron's rows. The same goes for "each memory its own table" [334]: you'd be typing every CREATE yourself forever, which is exactly the trap you're describing.

The seed is only the physical actions, about 7: now (a new moment), new (a neuron is born), lay (a shard), write (a memory), again (the same truth, count +1), end (a truth shifts), and forget (hippocampus only). Everything else (find, same, if, count, say) is a formula: a run of those, taught once, then hit by name. That's how you get to your 20 or 30 neurons without hand-laying them.

It works. In a throwaway db I laid one neuron whose action is "new" and hit it twice, meaning a step landed on it. Each hit made a timeline moment and a newborn neuron, and nobody typed INSERT INTO neuron:

neuron|1|||new    <- the one hand-laid neuron
neuron|2||1|      <- born at moment 1
neuron|3||2|      <- born at moment 2

The hit itself still comes from outside: you for now, later the ear or the beat, later another neuron's run.

Born, laid, written. I muddled "born". From now on: a neuron is born, a shard is laid, a memory is written. Your hop is right:
- A shard exists: hop along it.
- No shard, but a neuron already lives at that place: lay the shard. That's learned, so next time it's the first case.
- No shard and nothing lives there: a neuron is born, and the shard to it is laid.
- No remainder: the run ends there, and a memory is written (or its count goes up by 1 if it's the same truth).

The second and third cases are learning: a hop taken the long way once becomes a shard. You never lay a shard by hand.

.quit only closes the sqlite3 program; the db file stays as it is. SQLite can hold several db files open together (ATTACH) and let go of them (DETACH), so parts of the brain could live in separate files. Since a trigger can't attach, switching parts on and off would be the outside's job.

One thing is in the way, and it's my mistake. In step 1 I gave you gate TEXT NOT NULL. A newborn from "new" has no place yet. Numbers get theirs from the number line, but nothing places an action neuron until relationships exist. In the test I let the gate stay empty until the neuron is placed. SQLite's UNIQUE still allows many empty gates; I tested that too. On your db, that means rebuilding the neuron table without NOT NULL and with a does column. It holds one row, so it costs nothing today; later it would mean moving every row. This is option (b) from 342: a neuron is born unplaced and gets its gate when its place is known.
