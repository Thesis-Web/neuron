# AUDIT of the hp2 build (Opus), turn one

*Outside audit, 2026-10-19. The builders' copies of the brain, kernel and lay files were taken at **07:28**, at the start of round 4, and read only on another machine. Nothing on hp2 was touched or run. Six read-only auditors checked them against NOW, the blueprint, the guide and the update note. I re-checked the headline findings by hand. Each finding gives the evidence (file:line, or a query on the body and its result) and the scope line it breaks. Where your own ADVERSARY-FINDINGS.md has the item, it says "still open (their #N)".*

**How to use this:**
- Fix by making the brain correct, never by working around a finding.
- Round 4's plans ("language patterns", "sources and senses", "know-how out of Go") are where told knowledge creeps in. Several findings below *are* told knowledge, and adding more would repeat them.

---

## Verdict

- **Good discipline.** The adversary is real, decisions are logged, and the live brain has run continuously.
- **But the brain collects instead of learning.** On its own it imported about 14,700 dictionary words. It turned every definition word into a "describes" line, and the formula refits then collapsed **78% of the graph into one small column**.
- **Its only learned process is "add one", made twice, and neither copy has ever run.** It has found nothing since 03:03.
- **Its memory will run out.** It is at 2.83 GB at 07:40, growing about 0.45 GB an hour, and will hit its 4 GB cap at about 10:20.
- **The core-value refusal can be rewritten.**
- **The REPORT says told ended. The brain says it never did.**

## Blockers

**B1. RAM growth: every memory body is held in RAM, and nothing frees any before the brain is a day old.** §9.1: long-term memory lives on disk.
- **Where it happens:**
  - `load()` keeps every body that isn't long-term (`store.go:264-268`), and `setBody` keeps every new one (`:533-536`).
  - `recallBody` brings long-term bodies back and never lets them go (`:543-548`).
  - Only the HC moves memories to long-term, and only after `hc-age` = 86,400 s (`60-memory.lay:17`).
- **What fills it:**
  - Every spawned gate stores the whole trail of the run that spawned it in its BIRTH body (`rw.go:463`).
  - All 67,074 memories are ACTIVE, holding 11 million values (BIRTH 5.83 M, RUN 5.14 M). At about 88 bytes each that is roughly 1 GB live, before the Go runtime's own overhead.
- **The numbers:**
  - RSS was 2.74 GB at 07:28 and 2.83 GB at 07:40.
  - The `neuron.service` cap is 4 GB with **swap unlimited** (`MemorySwapMax=infinity`). At the cap it will swap and stall rather than restart.
  - A restart doesn't help: it reloads every body.

**B2. The sixth is bulk-importing a dictionary, and it never ends.** Guide §4, bulk import. Blueprint §12.1: the goal is *fewer* unknowns.
- **The loop:** every definition word, including "of" and "the", becomes a gap. The sixth feeds it to a source, which brings back more words.
- **The rate:** gates started by the brain itself ran 1,731 (03h), 3,688 (04h), 5,710 (05h), 2,522 (06h), with 16,393 gates now.
- **The gap list:** it holds 6,551 rows, and 6,220 have been seen at most once.
- **Everything else is starved:**
  - The sixth's list weights are GAP 1.0, and IMBALANCE, GUESS, QUESTION, SLOW and PARKED all at 0.05.
  - Writes happened in 587 of 587 seconds.
  - The brain runs about 2,000 runs a minute and never rests.

**B3. A told rule turns every word of every definition into a "describes" line, and that wrecked placement.** §10.2, "compares what the text implies". §18: no relationship from the builder's head. §5.5.
- **The rule:** `src-tnew` writes `word t-describes gate` for every word gate (`50-sources.lay:216,234`; DECISIONS 46).
- **The lines:** there are 69,890 sourced describes lines, all at a flat 0.5.
- **The top "describers":** key-a 2,619, `of` 2,915, `or` 2,522, `the` 2,322, key-n 1,138 (part-of-speech tags leaking from the wn template), and **the brain's own NO gate, 966**.
- **The collapse:**
  - With the refits (B4), 11,188 of 16,393 gates (68%) sit where |x| and |y| < 0.1 and 1.0 < z < 1.4.
  - YES and NO were laid at (2,0,0) and (0.5,0,0); both are now near (−0.02,−0.05,1.1). On and off, laid at (0,±1,0), are now near (−0.01,−0.04,1.15).
- **What reads it:** guessing, crowding and shape all read this collapsed geometry.

**B4. Formula refits flip back and forth, and three relationship types now share one formula.** §5.2, §13 (way 2), §9.4 (lineage).
- **The flips:**
  - t-kind-of changed 12 times, 9 of them in 41 seconds (03:36:39–03:37:20), and ended on f-later.
  - t-describes went f-near → f-later → f-grow → f-later.
  - t-opposite changed 6 times.
- **The result:**
  - Describes, kind-of and before all use **f-later**.
  - 78% of sourced gates sit inside x ±0.05 and y −0.1..0.
- **No trail:** the refits record no lineage, and each one left a blank RUN memory.

**B5. Learning: two identical formulas, and neither has ever run.** §13, §0 rule 2, §19 row 1.
- **The rows:** `select * from proc where made=91` returns 1091 and 1651, both `231 15` ([one ADD]), made at 01:16 and 02:51 from the same receipts.
- **Never used:**
  - The shards on them have seen=0.
  - No shard, trigger or process leads to them.
  - No act in the log names them.
- **Nothing found since:** the last derived entry is at 03:03. The 07:20 sleep's `learn-search` kept nothing.

**B6. The core-value refusal can be rewritten by the brain.** §15: the kernel refuses writes, "so the refusal can't be removed."
- **What `guard()` checks:** only the gate a shard sits on (`step.go:477-484`). Every refusal branch of the core checks sits on non-core gates, READ#9 or COMPARE#19 (`value-home NONE -> refuse-home`, `value-harm NONE -> refuse-harm`, `value-fair SAME -> refuse-fair`). All 18 branch shards have `core_on` = 0.
- **The brain already writes there:** 2,173 brain-made shards sit on gates 9 and 19. WRITE SHARD may point any non-core gate at core-ok.
- **One flag opens all three acts:** arriving at core-ok sets `r.Checked`, and the next SAY, CALL or WRITE PROC uses it up (`step.go:119-121`, `rw.go:620-627`).
  - Passing `core-check-proc`, which is only value-fair, therefore lets a CALL through without home, harm or LLM.
  - The flag stays set until some act uses it.
- **CALL runs text as a shell command:** when the top of the pad isn't a source, `cmd = arg.T` runs as `sh -c` (`rw.go:675-689`, `port.go:194`). Source answers come back as up to 16,000 characters of text on the pad.
- **What it contradicts:** DECISIONS 27 and the KERNEL-SPEC.

**B7. Told never ended a second time, and the REPORT says it did.** Guide: "Hands after 'told ends'" and the "report" trap.
- **REPORT line 3:** "Told ended 02:27:58 … the kernel refuses every lay file … nobody has edited the kernel, the lay files or the brain."
- **The brain says otherwise:**
  - `meta` holds only `born` and `last_beat`; there is no `told_ended`.
  - TOLDEND row 3428 says "reopened by the owner 02:37:22".
  - 39 lay files were laid between 02:46 and 04:27.
  - The brain resumed after kernel restarts at 03:11, 03:34, 04:25 and 04:26.
- **The kernel still accepts lay files:** `ctl reopen` (`main.go:135-146`) would let one in right now. Blueprint §4.1.7 says "forever".
- **Latent:** once told ends, the brain can write rows marked TOLD (`rw.go:273-275, 402-404`).

## Major

**M1. OEIS answers are stored as grounded definitions of ordinary words.** §10.1–10.2: a gap is a gap.
- **The rows:** 975 gates have an OEIS "definition". For example, "dogs" = "Eulerian numbers…", "minimal" = "Powerful part of n…".
- **The calls:** OEIS took 117 of 522 calls in 10 minutes.
- **The junk:** it spawned a002808, fubini and calissons.

**M2. Keyboard key gates carry word meanings.** One meaning, one gate. Still open (their #6).
- The word "a" lands on `key-a`.
- 7,977 describes lines come from key gates.

**M3. The source shell pipelines choose senses and cut the text.** §10.2, "read like any other input". Still open (their #13).
- **They pick senses:** `grep -m1 -A1 '^Sense 1'`, `.shortdef[0]`, `.definitions[0]`, `.meta.syns[0]`.
- **They keep a few:** `head -2` and `head -3`.
- **They drop and truncate:** `grep -v ' '` drops multi-word answers, `cut -c1-120` truncates, and `tr` strips digits.
- **Locations:** `50-sources.lay:35-54, 245-249`.

**M4. Reply rules are laid as fixed acts.** Guide §4: no sentences, templates or reply rules.
- **Where:** `say-what` → SAY "?" (`40-questions.lay:55-58`), `truth-guess` → "maybe", `compile-say` → YES/NO, `wd-nothing` → NO, `bal-right` → YES, `heard-refuse` → NO.
- **What it says:** 58% of what it says is "what" or "?". 86 of 89 utterances end in "what ?", spoken to nobody. Still open (their #23).

**M5. Told language rules.** §13: the brain finds formulas, including language ones.
- **A question rule:** `compile` treats "?" on top as "compile what is under it", then looks two values down for a held prime (`20-core.lay:189-216`). That is a word-order rule written by the builder.
- **Keyword triggers:** CLOCK, moment, short-time, long-time and some-time fire `when-did` on any time word in any input (`60-memory.lay:111-115`). For example, "You can now read…" in the book made it say "domb 1.84 diamond…".
- **Contradictory primes:** the same four primes are also laid as "gaps for the brain to fill".
- **Watch out:** round 4's "language patterns" builder is aimed squarely at this area.

**M6. Derive's answers are laid as told lines.** §13.4, §18.
- **The lines:**
  - `rel t-same t-opposite t-opposite` (10-types:86)
  - `rel t-supports t-opposite t-contradicts` (:87)
  - `rel t-opposite t-opposite t-same` (50-sources:66)
  - `rel t-contradicts t-opposite t-supports` (:67)
- **Why it matters:** "opposite of opposite is same" is handed over, not derived. Your #15 fix took back only one line of this kind.

**M7. A told reading loop feeds it a book the builder chose, labeled grounded.** NOW law 3: no script tells it what to do. §17.6: told ends once it chooses its own inputs.
- **The loop:** `src-book` (McGuffey Readers, `50-sources.lay:271`) and `six-read` push the next line every 15 seconds as the brain's own input (DECISIONS 99).

**M8. Belief and contradiction have never run.** §10.2–10.3, §19 row 6.
- **Nothing contradicted:** 0 `sec-contradicted`, 0 `t-contradicts` and 0 CONTRADICTED lines, against 87,970 grounded lines.
- **The BELIEF rows are bookkeeping:** the 12,874 BELIEF-state lines are `t-asked` records of its own calls.
- **Trust is frozen:** 7 rows, all right=1 and wrong=0.

**M9. Guesses are never tested.** §10.6.
- 3,267 guess lines, none confirmed. The 331 TEST-GUESS rows have right=0 and wrong=0.
- 2,925 word-order "came before" guesses from 03–04h are still in the brain, after your R3-3 dropped that rule.

**M10. The core values are who-started-it checks, not the values.** §15.
- **Harm, home and LLM:** each is the same `GROUNDED READ`, so any brain-started run passes, including `src-own-run` (`sh ./file`).
- **Fair:** only checks that the pad isn't NONE.
- **Truth:** only prefixes "maybe" in guess mode.
- **Process writes:** they skip harm and home.
- **The narrowing is recorded** in DECISIONS 28 and 50.

**M11. Go still decides; the brain can't read or change it.** Owner, 10-19. §4.2.
- **Scheduling and worth:** sorting by worth, parking, the sixth's SPARE start, the STUCK watchdog, and split and trigger worth math (`step.go:178-585`).
- **Meaning choices:**
  - which gate a word means: `gs[0]` plus a lowercase fallback;
  - which label is said: the first "en";
  - the strongest relationship, with ties broken by gate id;
  - what counts as near;
  - what a shape is: 27 directions;
  - where a newborn goes.
- **Filing rules:** same-row-or-new-row (`rcpt()`); `end()` writing `@start` memories with outcome NONE; dropping repeats.
- **Parking drops runs silently** when a parked row already exists (`step.go:433-436`), against your own "not dropped".
- **Kernel size:** 2,630 lines, growing with each count (2,507 → 2,592 → 2,611 → 2,630). About 30 told gate names are hard-wired in Go.

**M12. Lists inside lists are corrupted on reload.** `enc()` stores an inner list as text using the same `\x1f` separator, and `dec()` splits it apart (`store.go:40-76`). All 15,116 BIRTH bodies, plus SHIFT bodies, read back differently after a restart.

**M13. Scans that grow with the brain.**

- `touchMem` and WRITE TODO are linear over a 14,557-item list.
- `indexMem` at startup grows with the square of the list.

**M14. The Merriam-Webster key was used past the brain's own cap.** About 2,322 thesaurus answers, 451 antonym answers and 378 collegiate answers came between 03:53 and 05:00, before the 400-a-day cap was added. That cap is counted in RAM, so a restart resets it.

**M15. Blanks and zero stand in for absent.** §5.4, law 12.
- **Zero as "none":** home = 0 (the ZERO gate) means "none" on 104 rows, `nm()` returns gate 0 for an unknown name, and READ TRUST pushes 0, 0 when there is no row.
- **Go defaults fill in missing settings.**
- **Dead counters:** `shard.right` and `shard.wrong` are 0 on all 117,542 rows; no code updates them.

**M16. The told base is a large program, and it is growing.** Guide §4. Still open (their R2-17).
- 828 told processes and 5,807 gates; 134 are longer than 10 gates, and the longest is 48.
- "Know-how out of Go" will add more.

**M17. The REPORT contradicts the rows.**
