Hand laid, exactly

  • 14 physical actions: READ, WRITE, COMPARE, ADD, SUBTRACT, MOVE, FIND, SPLIT, JOIN, CLOCK, HEAR, SAY, CALL, SPAWN. These are the silicon. Nothing in the brain can make them.
  • 16 physics tables, each with its trigger: input, encoder, decide, lay, born, source, agree, end, sense, memory, timeline, say, BUILD, formula, var, state. A trigger cannot CREATE, so the first of these cannot be born; they are laid once, as one block.
  • mother and source: two functions loaded beside SQLite. mother runs SQL text. source reaches outside and brings text back.
  • The primes' actions: when a prime is born by typing, what it does is told, from the table on the page. That is the one told meaning layer.
  • Three silicon jobs (added in version 8, from the worksheet test): not inverts the yes or no in hand; flip goes to the other end of the axis a word sits on (less becomes more, the sign turns); shift writes at a later time a state different from the one read earlier. The worksheet's one minus was doing all three. They are operations with names, like find and read; whether any English word is one of them is for its sources and its tests to show.
  • Eight primitive runs, one physical action each: find, read, compare, join, split, say, write, move. And the physics neurons by name: a formula may name memory, state, source, sense, agree, timeline, or the roots SPLIT and JOIN; landing on one runs that neuron's own trigger on the words after it. So a word's expression can point at a memory recall, a state read, a source check, a balance, a diff, a timeline row or a question run that branches, and every one of those is a table with its own trigger that the brain can rewrite. Version 8 had these as eight separate functions; that was canned, and it is gone.
  • One source more: the human body-channel rows (Lancaster norms, 39,707 words, this slice: the page's words). It says which of eleven channels a word lights in a person: eyes, ears, skin, gut, nose, tongue, hand, mouth, head, foot, torso. It is attached as rows, like a dictionary; it is an input perspective and a comparison, not a meaning.

Everything else on the stage is born by typing: every word gate, every shard, every hop trigger, every relation, every meaning, every process. A process arrives taught, by one typed line; after three memories of the same receipt it is learned and has a shortcut; after that it is a hop. That is the arc from told to automated, and the point of connection is when nothing is left on the absent list.

The processes are the words

  • There is no process called qlike. There is the word like. When a run passes it, its formula fires: like = write :head relation. A definition row "like shop" runs with store as its subject, passes like, and like writes store alike shop. opposite and kind work the same way. derive and deduce are words too: born fires them on a new word, and typing "derive the opposite of below" fires derive on its object.
  • A word's meaning is its formula. word = ... is the only told form. Primes only: the word runs those actions when passed. Holes and primitive runs: a formula. Words starting with a colon are holes filled from the run that passes the word: :head, :arrival, :rest, and :subject, :verb, :object cut at the first being word. find, read, compare, join, split, say, write, move are primitive runs, one physical action each over the words after them. A formula may name another word with a formula, and it fires inside.
  • The trigger is a column on the DNA row. By default a row fires the word's own formula. what are -> who lays, or updates, the row in what for remainder are so it fires who instead. who is that -> about does it in the being verb itself, keyed by what came before and what comes after. So who is one neuron with many rows, and the remainder picks the row, and the row picks what runs, where it looks, what it says.
  • Learning is the same for all of them. A word's formula is taught by one line, learned after three memories of the same receipt, automated after that. Click any orange dot and read its formula and its rows.

Two parts to a meaning, and nothing assigned

  • What it is made of is a line: the word on the left, words on the right. What it does is the descent: the run that passes the word fires its row, the row fires the words behind it, and so on down until physical actions fire. The card shows both, with the depth and the words that have nothing behind them yet.
  • No line on this page is a meaning I picked. The shop lines are gone; they were me assigning. What stays is mechanism: the physics, the primes as words, the operators as words, and a few lines that say what a mechanism word does to the rows (like writes a relation; who looks in state). Those are labeled as mine and are to be replaced by what tests show.
  • Operators are neurons, from your roots worksheet: minus, plus, a lot more, twice, the inverse. Each has DNA rows keyed by what came before and what comes after. When a formula is taught, the minus in it gets a row for that context; the row's trigger column picks the job: not, flip, shift, or the default (flip). below - above -> flip binds the row in minus for arrival below and remainder above. So the same minus can mean three different things in one sentence, by row, which is what you said; and the three jobs are three different physical actions, which is what the worksheet test showed. Slash is a part and adds SPLIT, star adds JOIN, plus adds ADD. divide and slash as two spellings of one gate is the encoder's job.
  • Hops carry operators too, by row. A hop operator between two words gets a row in the operator's own neuron, keyed by the arrival and the next word; the trigger column can name a mechanic or a word with a formula, so the same star can do different things in different places. Each hop event says what it acted on: the silicon, the graph, or both. A hop is a plus by default: continue. Typed between two words, - steps the run back one gate, so the next word is hit from the earlier arrival and a different row fires. * forks a test run with the same remainder, arriving as test, and compares its receipt with the main run's at the end. / is a choice: the path with an existing row is taken, otherwise the path is taken as a guess and the output says so. ** and sqrt have no mechanics yet and say so. These are my proposals for what an operator hop does to the run, and nothing about what any word means.
  • Heard is not said. A typed line's first row arrives from heard. "Say the last answer back" feeds the brain's own words to its ear, arriving from said: a different first row in the same neuron, and I and you swap when a formula looks in state.

How to read the stage

  • White dots at the center are the physical actions. The ring around them is the physics; the violet dots inside it are the operators and the three silicon jobs. Gold dots are primes. Green dots are meanings a word reached. Orange dots have a formula. Every point is a number; the word on it is its label.
  • The dim grey dots on the outermost shell are labels still in the encoder: no meaning, no formula, no relation. They are drawn there for the eye only and have no place of their own. A point gets its place, and moves, when a relation line, a formula or a meaning pulls it; that move is the graph changing. In version 8 I put these labels in a flat grid beside the stage, which flattened the whole picture; that is undone.
  • Thin white lines are hop triggers the brain laid: the stored way from gate to gate. Blue, red and purple lines are relationships the questions wrote; pale green lines are forms the encoder recognised (cars beside car, bigger on an arc from big). Colored lines are runs; the bright one is the run at this tick.
  • When a row lands, its gate pulses, and its meaning flashes down to the actions it runs. The ledger counts them.
