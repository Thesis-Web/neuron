## What each source holds now, and how a row is shaped

A row is (word, hw, text): the lookup key (lowercased, as since the slice), the headword as written, the text that returns to the brain as one return run. Labels in square brackets are the shape the slice already had for WordNet's synonyms (`[syn: {a}, {b}]`, as dict.org prints them); every other label is the source's own name for the thing, from its documentation.

**wn** (WordNet 3.1): 213,224 rows, 152,557 words, 68.6 MB of text. One row per (lemma, synset), multi-word lemmas included (the brain cannot ask them with one symbol; they are there to be found): `noun 1: gloss [syn: {..}] [hypernym: {..}] [hyponym: {..}] ... [frames: ..] [examples: ..] [lexicographer file: noun.Tops] [sense key: ..] [tag count: N]`. The sense number and tag count are from `index.sense`; the pointer names are WordNet's (`wninput(5WN)`): ant (as dict.org), hypernym, instance hypernym, hyponym, instance hyponym, member/substance/part holonym and meronym, attribute, derivationally related form, domain of synset topic/region/usage, member of this domain topic/region/usage, entailment, cause, also see, verb group, similar to, participle of verb, pertainym, derived from adjective. A lexical pointer (an antonym, a derivation) stands on the row of the lemma it belongs to and names the one target lemma; a semantic pointer stands on every row of the synset and names every lemma of the target. Verb frames from `verb.Framestext`; the example sentences from `sents.vrb` with the lemma in the `%s` slot; the adjective markers `(a) (p) (ip)` kept on the lemma. The exception lists (`noun.exc` etc.) as rows keyed by the inflected form: `[noun exceptions: aardwolves aardwolf]`. Left out: `log.grind.3.1` (the build log), `dbfiles/` (the same synsets in the lexicographer's input form), `cntlist.rev` (the same counts in another order), `index.*` (an index of what is in the rows), the synset offsets (ids, resolved into the target lemmas).

**gcide** (GCIDE 0.54, Webster 1913 with additions): 243,762 rows, 114,774 words, 199 MB of text. One row per paragraph of the raw (`<p>...</p>`, GCIDE's own unit), for each `<ent>` word of the entry the paragraph belongs to, in file order; the 9 blocks of text that sit outside every paragraph (a factbook extract under Pakistan, a table under a J entry) as rows of the entry above them; 11 entries that begin with `<hw>` and have no `<ent>` keyed by the headword without its marks. Every tag with semantic content becomes `[label: content]` with the label from `tagset.txt` (headword, pronunciation, part of speech, verb forms, conjugated form, etymology, sense, definition, mark, field, quotation, quotation author, authority, synonyms, antonyms, note, usage, entry reference, collocation, collocation definition, alternative name, alternative spelling, plural, species name, genus, family, source, and the rest, 190 in all, in `gcide_tags.py`); a tag that is typography only (italic, bold, small caps, the italic marker of the headword inside an example or a quotation, the italic marker of a foreign word inside an etymology, right justification, table layout, font sizes) loses the tag and keeps its text. Entities (`<amac/` ā, `<ae/` æ, `<hand/` ☞, the Greek letters, the fractions) become their Unicode characters from `webfont.txt`; the diacritic entities as the letter with its combining mark; `<?/` (the typists' mark for an unreadable character) as `(?)`; the `\'d8` escape (the bar before a word taken unchanged from a foreign language) as ‖. Left out: the `<-- p. 345 -->` page-number comments (where the printed page began; not the dictionary's text). The paragraph that lists 1,179 words formed with Un- is laid 1,179 times, once under each word: 115 MB of the 199. (The morning's load gave only the prefix those definitions.)

**wikt** (Wiktionary, the kaikki.org extract of the English Wiktionary, 2026-10-29 download): 3,607,594 rows, 1,361,252 words, 1.35 GB of text, 1,677 MB file. For each of the 1,499,161 entries (a word with one part of speech and one etymology): a head row (`[word: ..] [part of speech: ..] [etymology N] [head: the head line] [forms: ..] [etymology: the text] [etymology templates: expansions not already in the text] [pronunciation: IPA, enPR, rhymes, homophones, with their tags] [hyphenation: ..] [categories: names] [wikipedia: ..] [wikidata: ..]`), one row per sense (`pos N: the glosses with their labels [glosses] [tags] [raw tags] [topics] [qualifier] [examples: text, ref, type] [synonyms] [antonyms] [hypernyms] [hyponyms] [meronyms] [holonyms] [troponyms] [coordinate terms] [related] [derived] [alt of] [form of] [attestations] [senseid] [categories] [wikipedia] [wikidata]`), a row per group of translations of a sense (`[translations of pos N (sense): language: word | roman | alt | tags | note | english; ...]`, 41% of the file's bytes), and a row per list of related words at the entry level. Every item of a list is its main value, then its other fields as `field: value` with kaikki's own field names. **Left out as markup or machinery, listed so the owner can object:** `links` (the wiki links of a gloss, the same words again), `bold_text_offsets` (byte positions), `id` (machine ids), `_dis` and `_dis1` (the extractor's disambiguation weights), `head_templates` names and arguments (their expansion stays), `etymology_templates` names and arguments (their expansions stay where the etymology text does not already hold them), `etymology_links` (link targets), category `kind`, `source`, `parents`, `orig`, `langcode` (the name stays), `audio`, `ogg_url`, `mp3_url` (audio file names and URLs), `lang` and `lang_code` of the entry (every entry is English), `code` and `lang_code` of a translation or descendant (the language name stays), `info_templates`, and a `translation` field that repeats an `english` field beside it.
