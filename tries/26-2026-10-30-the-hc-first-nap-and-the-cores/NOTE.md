## The cores: what holds and what does not, measured tonight

Everything runs on one core now because SQLite, with one table per neuron, punishes a second connection for every birth: it reloads the whole schema (22,000 tables) each time, and the first v7's second connection starved for the write lock and died. So the way to four cores is four **files** with four **processes**, talking only by mail: a sender writes into the receiver's inbox table in the receiver's file (attaching it), and **nobody attaches main** except main (its births stay its own; the sixth, the guesser and the hc birth rarely, so attaching their files is cheap). Main reads the others' outboxes between its batches. This means the sixth must be retrained to work from mail: today its observing line reaches into main's run sheet and seq; in the mail form the `seen` letter carries the run's sheet (the empty cells, the unknown list) and the sixth answers with lines. The guesser already has the mail shape (inbox, outbox) and could take the GPU later (the owner's 10-28 words). The hc is mail-shaped from the start.

Two constraints to clear first, the owner's: this SQLite attaches ten databases at most and the brain holds ten (the sixth, eight sources, the guesser; mw is out), so the hc has no slot until either SQLite is built with a higher `SQLITE_MAX_ATTACHED` and preloaded into the door's process (I was not permitted to fetch and build it tonight), or the three small hand-laid sources (map, sqldoc, measured) share one file. And four brains made the same, each defining a share of the unknowns, would birth their own gate numbers; what one brain can hand another is not its rows but the lines that made them (the define line, the returns as text from the sources, the known and unknown as symbols), which the receiver lays again as its own. That is the "help with the cascade" that is possible: the sources' asking and the cutting of returns are the cheap part; the laying is each brain's own. Whether that saves time is open and not measured.
