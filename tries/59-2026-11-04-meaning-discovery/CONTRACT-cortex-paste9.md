# CORTEX BUILD CONTRACT
## NEURON — Autonomous Discovery of Operational Meaning

### 1. Mission
Develop and verify Neuron's ability to discover the operational meaning of words from its existing knowledge, definitions, relationships, usage evidence, historical runs, and contextual modifiers. Neuron must derive how a meaning operates within the existing brain rather than having a developer manually assign an executable procedure to each word. The required result is a reusable, general mechanism through which Neuron can progressively discover more complex operational meanings from previously grounded meanings and operations. This must remain consistent with Neuron's graph-native execution architecture. The objective is not to build an English vocabulary, another parser, a lookup table of word-to-function mappings, or a collection of handcrafted word implementations. The objective is to discover, express, test, and retain executable meaning.

### 2. Governing architecture
2.1. Word and identity. A written word is a label, not its complete meaning. One neuron may contain multiple DNA shards representing distinct contextual meanings or uses. A complex meaning is not automatically a new neuron. Its operational behavior may be represented through the appropriate DNA shard and its relationships, triggers, and executable continuations. Do not create a new point simply because multiple root operations combine to describe a word.
2.2. DNA-shard execution. Meaning execution is governed by the DNA shard selected for the actual run. For ordinary input runs, arrival and remainder participate in determining the appropriate continuation. Contextual subruns may resolve the applicable shard where more information is required. The selected shard invokes the applicable graph-native operations through its existing trigger and continuation mechanisms. Do not replace these mechanisms with a hardcoded word dispatcher or an external instruction interpreter.
2.3. Operational domains. A meaning may operate upon any combination of: silicon and kernel-provided execution physics; graph nodes, DNA shards, relationships, positions, and traversal; existing database operations; state and state transitions; time and timelines; memories and historical runs; events and their outcomes; other meanings and their executable compositions; external source requests through Mother; evidence, trust, belief, knowledge, and uncertainty. No single domain is assumed to contain the entire meaning.
2.4. Kernel boundary. The native C kernel provides the deterministic execution physics. The graph provides the learned operations, relationships, contextual choices, and executable compositions. Mother transports requests and results to and from supported external destinations. It must not become an external meaning engine. Do not move cognitive decision-making into external Python, shell scripts, SQL dispatch rules, or additional application-level C logic. SQL may be executed through the existing graph-to-Mother pathway when requested by graph-native operations. SQL execution must not become a substitute for meaning discovery.

### 3. Existing capabilities to preserve
The owner reports that the current architecture supports automated neuron construction, DNA construction, definition sourcing, relationships, memory, timeline, state, historical events, graph-native triggers, and internally spawned runs. Cortex also supplies persistent project context, judgments, trust history, Blink, and hydration. These capabilities are prerequisites, not items to rebuild. The builder must inspect existing artifacts and receipts to determine what is implemented and working. Unsupported status claims are not acceptable. Do not replace or duplicate existing mechanisms to make the current experiment easier.

### 4. Meaning-discovery hypothesis
The working hypothesis is: the operational meaning of a word can be derived incrementally from its definition, relationships to grounded meanings, observed usage, applicable context, and already available executable operations. Relevant evidence may include: source definitions and their provenance; words and meanings appearing within those definitions; previously grounded operational meanings; relationships, similarity, opposition, analogy, and other established graph relations; historical examples of actual use; prefixes, suffixes, morphology, and grammatical roles; tense, aspect, mood, and modality; input modality and perspective; state, time, and event history; arrival conditions, remainder, triggers, and context; historical runs and their observed results; independent observations or verifiable source evidence. These are potential inputs to discovery, not a mandatory checklist or a predefined scoring formula. The builder must investigate which relationships actually contribute to correct operational interpretations. The discovery rule must emerge from supported cases and executable evidence, not from manually assigning actions to example words. Definitions are evidence. They do not automatically prove an operational mapping.

### 5. Additive learning requirement
Begin with operations whose actual behavior is already grounded and verified. The brain must investigate compositions of these operations to discover more complex meanings. The general progression is: known operation → supported relationship → candidate composition → contextual application → actual execution → observed result → validation → retained operational meaning. A candidate may be incomplete, uncertain, incorrect, or inapplicable. The system must be capable of rejecting unsupported interpretations instead of manufacturing one. Successful discoveries may subsequently support the discovery of further meanings. The actual formula structure, variable relationships, and selection rules are subjects of discovery and evaluation, not owner-prescribed answers.

### 6. Explicitly prohibited substitutions
1. Creating one point per word or one point per combined operation simply because an example was supplied. 2. Manually building a list of proposed root words and declaring them the foundational meaning system. 3. Quoting definitions onto points and calling that executable meaning. 4. Hardcoding a selected English word to a predetermined SQL or C operation. 5. Creating names such as READ_FIND as substitutes for discovering an English meaning. 6. Using a generic WORD + WORD concatenation rule and treating the result as semantic discovery. 7. Preauthoring the formula for the test word and then demonstrating that the formula executes. 8. Moving interpretation, planning, or word-selection decisions to an external program. 9. Constructing canned sentence responses or a separate natural-language interpreter to simulate semantic understanding. 10. Treating successful execution of manually authored root actions as proof of autonomous meaning discovery. 11. Altering unrelated kernel physics, historical data, or existing mechanisms to accommodate a demonstration. 12. Creating visual geometry, artificial attraction layers, or graph shapes that do not participate in the real operational computation. 13. Treating successful retrieval, dictionary matching, or a correct verbal explanation as proof of executable meaning. Examples supplied by the owner or another LLM are illustrative evidence only. They are not instructions to instantiate the example.

### 7. Required development sequence
Phase A, establish the actual starting state: inspect the existing kernel, DNA, sources, relationships, triggers, historical runs, and current test-brain experiments; identify which primitive operations are genuinely executable and which meanings remain descriptive or unresolved; identify existing facilities for candidate discovery, contextual selection, validation, and graph modification; do not invent missing facilities.
Phase B, discover candidate meaning compositions: select unresolved, source-grounded meanings using a documented selection method; examine their definitions, existing relationships, contextual examples, and available operational primitives; derive candidate executable compositions from those materials; preserve the exact evidence used in the derivation; candidate interpretations must not be taken from the owner's illustrative formulas as predetermined answers.
Phase C, establish contextual discrimination: investigate at least one word with materially distinct uses or meanings; determine whether existing DNA-shard selection mechanisms can distinguish those uses through arrival, remainder, context, relationships, and applicable modifiers; do not solve this by introducing a new neuron for each sense unless the actual architecture and evidence independently require distinct identities.
Phase D, execute and validate: execute candidate meanings within the isolated test brain; capture actual graph runs, selected DNA shards, triggered operations, state changes, memory changes, outputs, and failures; compare observed results against independently specified tests; do not equate a successful text response with successful physical execution.
Phase E, discover a transferable rule: study multiple successful and unsuccessful derivations; determine whether a common operational pattern can explain their results; test that pattern against additional unresolved meanings which were not used to construct it; a rule that works only on the original authored examples does not qualify.
Phase F, retain validated learning: where a derived operation is supported, record its meaning, applicability, dependencies, provenance, uncertainty, and tested outcomes using the existing graph facilities; if a more compact executable formula is learned, verify that its behavior is equivalent to the validated longer process over the tested applicability domain; preserve historical evidence and prior states.

### 8. Required expected outputs
EO-01 Discovery: at least one previously unresolved operational meaning is derived without the builder writing its final procedure into the graph in advance.
EO-02 Correct representation: the derived meaning is represented through the appropriate existing neuron/DNA architecture, not an invented word-function mapping layer.
EO-03 Context discrimination: a polysemous or context-sensitive word selects different appropriate executable behavior under different valid contexts.
EO-04 Physical operation: the selected meaning causes observable and correct operations in the test brain. Merely displaying a matching definition is insufficient.
EO-05 Evidence traceability: the system retains a trace of which definitions, relationships, prior meanings, use cases, historical runs, and observations supported the derivation.
EO-06 Generalization: the discovered mechanism applies successfully to at least one different unresolved meaning whose operational solution was not embedded in the original examples.
EO-07 Uncertainty and failure: unsupported or contradictory interpretations remain unresolved, qualified, or rejected. The system must not invent an executable meaning merely to produce an answer.
EO-08 Architectural integrity: existing kernel physics, graph execution, source routing, histories, and accepted functionality remain operational. No new external cognitive layer is introduced.
EO-09 Reproducibility: the relevant construction and execution can be reproduced from documented starting state and inputs, yielding the same accepted semantics and operational outcomes.
EO-10 Learning retained: a validated discovered meaning remains available to future runs without replaying the developer's original instructions or manually reconstructing its process.

### 9. Cortex evaluation requirements
Cortex must evaluate progress using its existing authority, provenance, trust, and historical-state systems. Evaluate separately: architectural compliance; factual correctness; executable correctness; progress toward expected outputs; evidence quality and source independence; regression against previously accepted behavior; builder reliability by domain and historical performance. A favorable result in one category must not conceal failure in another. Architectural invariants are hard acceptance conditions, not performance metrics that can be traded away. Preserve source-specific and topic-specific trust, existing source authority floors and ceilings, and applicable missing-source normalization. Where evidence conflicts, record the conflict and establish a discriminating test if one is possible. Do not approve a change simply because the proposing LLM claims it is correct.

### 10. Completion conditions
This contract is complete only when the expected outputs have been independently evaluated and the evidence establishes a reusable discovery capability. An isolated demonstration may be recorded as a partial milestone. It must not be promoted to the live brain merely because manually authored roots execute or a scripted example produces the expected output. If the discovery mechanism cannot yet be built, report the exact missing prerequisite, supporting evidence, and smallest experiment needed to resolve it. Do not replace the missing capability with a simulated success.

### 11. Mandatory build receipts
For every completed work unit, retain: starting state and test-brain identity; specific expected output being addressed; actual modifications and affected files or graph records; inputs, sources, and provenance; DNA selections, executed runs, and observable effects; expected versus actual results; pass, fail, or unresolved status; regressions and contract violations; ending state and what has actually been learned. All states must remain distinguishable as demonstrated, inferred, hypothesized, or not yet tested.
Final acceptance principle: the builder does not satisfy this contract by writing meanings for Neuron. The builder satisfies it by demonstrating that Neuron can discover, test, and retain operational meanings using its own existing execution architecture.
