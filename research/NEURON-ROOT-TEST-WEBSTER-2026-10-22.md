## 3. Does the dictionary close onto the primes?
The 60 most-used non-prime defining words (used, often, man, come, great, another ...) have entries in which only 241 of 6892 content tokens are primes (3.5%). One level down from the primes, the text is ordinary vocabulary, not primes. A raw dictionary does not bottom out in the primes within a few levels; its entries are full of examples, etymology and quotation. The descent needs the defining sense extracted, not the whole entry.

## 4. A content kernel
The smallest set of non-function words such that every prime entry holds at least two of them, by greedy cover: 10 words: used, often, man, place, part, make, great, upon, power, match. This is dictionary style (used, often, upon), not meaning. It says the kernel of Webster's entries is how dictionaries are written.

## 5. What this does and does not show
- It shows which primes carry the others in a real dictionary, and that function words carry everything.
- It does not show what any root does to the silicon. That is the next test, and it runs in the brain, by combining candidates with the operators and reading the rows.
